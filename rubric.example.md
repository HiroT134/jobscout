\# Job scoring rubric



\## Target roles (strong fit)

\- Business Analyst or Senior Business Analyst at logistics, retail or utilities companies

\- Process Analyst / Systems Analyst roles with requirements gathering in the title or description

\- Business Analyst at software or digital product companies, unless the title is graduate or Head of / Director-level

\- Data-focused analyst roles that sit in a business team (for example Reporting Analyst, Insights Analyst), unless the title is graduate-level



\## Acceptable (medium fit)

\- Product Owner roles at mid-size Auckland companies

\- Test Analyst or UAT Lead roles with stakeholder-facing content

\- Change Analyst roles in operational teams



\## Poor fit (skip)

\- Graduate, intern and coordinator titles

\- Pure software engineering, data engineering or sales roles

\- Head of, Director, GM and other executive-level roles

\- A role that matches a poor-fit rule is skip even if its title also matches a target role



\## Consulting roles

\- Analyst roles at consulting firms are maybe

\- Partner, Director and pre-sales roles at consulting firms are skip

\- "Expression of interest" postings (no specific role) are skip



\## Industries

\- Preferred: logistics, retail, utilities, agritech

\- Neutral: government, education, consulting

\- Avoid: gambling and tobacco



\## Pay and terms

\- Minimum base: 100k NZD permanent

\- Contract: minimum 600 NZD per day

\- Alert emails rarely show pay. If pay is not stated, treat it as neutral: do not guess a figure and note "pay unknown" in the reasons.



\## Recruiter-posted roles

\- When a recruitment agency posts for an unnamed client, judge the role on its title and treat the unknown employer as neutral



\## Location

\- Auckland, hybrid or remote. Roles in other cities are skip unless fully remote.



\## Seniority signals

\- Positive: "Senior", "Lead"

\- Negative: "Graduate", "Intern", "Coordinator"



\## Output

Score 0 to 100, using the full range. Verdict: shortlist (70+), maybe (40 to 69), skip (below 40).

Give 2 to 3 short reasons. If the email lacks the data to judge (pay, scope), say so

rather than guessing.
